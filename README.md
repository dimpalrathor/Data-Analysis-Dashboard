# Sample-Superstore-Data Analysis (Interactive Dashboard creation using Python + Streamlit)
## Project Objective
The Superstore wants to create an annual sales report. So that, the owner of the the Superstore can understand  their customers and grow more sales.


## Dataset used
- <a href ="https://github.com/dimpalrathor/Data-Analysis-Dashboard/blob/main/Sample%20-%20Superstore.xls">Dataset</a>


# Questions (KPIs)
-Total Sales over selected periods and across filters (Region, State, City, Category).

-Total Profit and profit margins per Region, State, City, and Category.

-Quantity sold per Category and Sub-category, tracked by month and year.

-Sales growth or decline trends month-over-month and year-over-year.

-Number of Orders processed in selected time frames and locations.

-Category-wise Sales distribution (e.g., Electronics, Furniture, Office Supplies).

-Region-wise Sales share (percentage of sales from each region).

-Segment-wise Sales (e.g., Consumer, Corporate, Home Office).

-Usage stats for data export/download buttons (e.g., number of downloads per category, region, or full dataset).

# Process
•	Simplify complex datasets into interactive and accessible visualizations such as charts, graphs, and tables for users to interpret quickly.

•	Monitor and evaluate key business metrics and KPIs in real time so teams can assess performance and react promptly to changing trends.

•	Uncover trends, patterns, and anomalies within business operations, helping identify areas for intervention and improvement.

•	Aggregate and compare data from multiple sources, reducing manual reporting time and increasing overall operational efficiency.

# Project Insight
Category-wise Sales: Bar and pie charts reveal which product categories (such as Office Supplies, Furniture, Technology) contribute most to overall revenues, helping identify areas of strength and opportunities for growth.

Region-wise Sales: Regional visualizations show the geographic spread of sales, allowing users to pinpoint high-performing and underperforming markets for targeted interventions.
Segment-wise Sales: Pie charts illustrate the distribution between Consumer, Corporate, and Home Office segments, enabling targeted customer strategies.

Time Series Trends: The dashboard’s time series analysis uncovers monthly sales peaks and troughs, revealing seasonality, growth periods, and forecasting opportunities.

Scatter Plot (Sales vs. Profit): This visualization identifies profitable and loss-making orders, showing the relationship between sales volumes and generated profits. It helps discover outliers and guides corrective actions for unprofitable transactions.

The dashboard empowers business leaders to quickly detect sales and profit trends, evaluate category/region/segment performance, and uncover both growth opportunities and operational inefficiencies.

# Final Conclusion:
Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) is a process to explore and understand your data without strong prior hypotheses. It uses statistical summaries and visualizations to reveal patterns, anomalies, relationships, and structure in the data. 

The goal is not (yet) to prove a model or causal claim, but to discover interesting features, guide further analysis, and validate assumptions. 

Dashboards built from EDA serve as interactive visual summaries. They help stakeholders visually grasp high-level and detailed insights (e.g. by region, category, time).
